Rules of play

Check every post

Run each poster against these before it goes out. If a rule and a reference disagree, the rule wins.

Do

  • One number, one player, one motion device. That is the whole hierarchy.
  • Keep it dark. Navy or Deep on most posts; Green for match days, Blue for team sheets.
  • Break a frame on every post. A window, a tile, a numeral or the headline.
  • Set every fact in Mono, caps, with "+" or "·" between items.
  • Write scores the cricket way: 104* (58), 3/18 (4), 18.4 ov.

Don't

  • No white grounds. Chalk is for type only.
  • No white text on green (1.9:1) and no blue text on navy (2.5:1). Deep goes on green; blue is a shape colour.
  • No blue kits with orange or saffron trim. They read as India's shirt. Kits are navy with green trim.
  • No red, orange or purple in season artwork, and no full-colour gradient logo on a poster.
  • No emoji, hashtags or exclamation marks on the artwork. Scale does the shouting.

Open before production

Team kits

Every frame uses one stand-in kit: navy with green trim. Swap in real team colours once the Season 2 kits are confirmed; navy and green stay the league's colours.

Country colours

Navy with green trim doesn't match a national side. An all-green kit would read as Pakistan or Bangladesh, so keep green to trim and graphics.

Real players

All faces are AI stand-ins. Shoot real players full body on a plain light-grey wall with hard light, to match the cut-out brief.

Text inside frames

Several generated frames carry AI-set words and invented placeholder names (the team sheet, the squad line-up). Reset all copy in the real fonts before anything is posted.
